Four small steps. A whole forest, eventually.

No flashcard decks to grind through. Just a root, a tree, and a habit that adds up.

1

Plant a root

Every lesson starts with one word root: a small, real piece of English with a clear meaning, like "spec" (to look) or "bene" (good).

2

Watch it grow

Learn the words that share that root (spectator, inspect, benefit, benevolent) and watch your root grow into a full tree, branch by branch.

3

Build your forest

Finished trees join your forest: a visual record of every root you've mastered, not just a number going up.

4

Never forget it

Words come back for review right before you'd naturally forget them, using spaced repetition under the hood.

Real roots. Real words. No guesswork.

Every root is hand-verified, not auto-generated, so the words it grows are ones you can trust.

dict
"to say, to speak" (Latin)

Grows into predict, dictate, and verdict.

spect
"to look, to watch" (Latin)

Grows into spectator, inspect, and perspective.

bene
"well, good" (Latin)

Grows into benefit, benevolent, and benediction.

tele
"far, distant" (Greek)

Grows into telephone, television, and telegraph.

Some words grow on two trees at once.

"Benediction" comes from both bene (good) and dict (to speak), so it renders as a real branch connecting those two trees in your forest, right on screen. It's proof the roots actually connect, not just a trivia note in a sidebar.
