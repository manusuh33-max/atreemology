How Atreemology works

Four ideas, working together: a root, a tree, a forest, and spaced repetition. Here's what each one actually does.

1. Plant a root

Every lesson starts with one word root — a small, real piece of English that carries a clear meaning but can't stand on its own as a word, like spect ("to look, to watch") or bene ("well, good"). You're not memorizing a list of thirty words; you're learning the one thing that unlocks all of them at once.

Roots are grouped into tiers: 10 standalone roots to start, then 12 that combine with another root into a shared word, then 4 hub roots that bridge to several others at once. You always start with the simple ones.

2. Watch it grow, branch by branch

As you learn the words that share a root — predict, dictate, verdict for dict — each one renders as a real branch on the tree, not a checkbox on a list. A root is a bound piece of meaning, so it lives underground as the taproot; every actual word, no matter how basic, grows above ground where you can see it.

A tree moves through real growth stages as you learn more of its words — seed, sprout, branches, leaves, flowering, complete — so you can see exactly how close a root is to fully grown.

3. Some words grow on two trees

Word roots aren't independent — a lot of English vocabulary is built from more than one at a time, and Atreemology shows that directly.

benediction
bene (good) + dict (to speak)

Renders as a branch physically connecting the bene tree to the dict tree.

teleportation
tele (far) + port (to carry)

Connects the tele tree to the port tree — tele alone bridges to four different roots.

inspect
in- (into) + spect (to look)

Even prefixes get their own small tree — spect alone bridges to six of them.

4. Build a forest, never forget what's in it

A finished tree doesn't disappear — it joins your forest for good, and spaced repetition keeps its words fresh without you having to think about it.

🌳

A permanent forest

Every root you finish stays visible in your forest — a growing, visual record of real vocabulary work, not a score that can reset.

⏱️

Spaced repetition, transparently

Rate a word again, hard, good, or easy, and it comes back in 1, 3, 7, or 14 days — right before you'd naturally forget it.

🧭

A field journal

Every root planted and word learned is logged automatically, so you can look back and see exactly what you've built.

🏅

Milestones that mean something

Achievements mark real progress — your first tree, your first ten roots, your first full grove — not arbitrary daily logins.
